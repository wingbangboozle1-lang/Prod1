A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

By The Countertop Guide editorial team · Listing-based guide · Listing capture date 2026-11-19 · No hands-on testing claimed

A quart figure is a starting point, not a serving-size guarantee. Before comparing numbers, check whether they describe a single cavity, split baskets, or a seller's food example.

On this page: Products in this guide · Full comparison · Questions

1. Keep the title and product-detail fields separate

Two historical listing captures show different capacity wording in the title and the product field. Do not silently convert or choose the number that looks more favorable: the Chefman dual-basket title says 6 quart while its field says 6 liters; the Emeril title says 26QT while its field says 24.6 liters. 2. Ask what the capacity describes

Two 3-quart baskets are split cooking zones. An 8-quart single basket is one cavity. A French-door oven has a different shape again. A total volume does not tell you the size of a tray, how much food fits in one layer, or whether two foods can be cooked together.

3. Treat serving and food-size examples as listing claims

The Emeril listing capture mentions 4–6 people, a 6-pound rotisserie chicken, and a 12-inch pizza. Those are seller examples, not a tested serving count. If one of those tasks matters to you, check the live listing, included accessories, and any current manual before buying.

4. Measure the outside and leave room for use

Compare the stated exterior dimensions with your counter, then check door swing, handles, ventilation, and the manufacturer's clearance instructions. Those practical constraints are not answered by the quart number. For dimension and footprint notes, see the side-by-side comparison.

5. Write down what is missing

For these captured listings, the Emeril wattage and two thermometer dimensions were not recorded. Missing details stay blank rather than being estimated. See the open evidence list.

Questions

Is 6 quarts the same as 6 litres?

No. The historical Chefman listing capture uses 6 Quart in the title and 6 liters in a product field. We preserve both labels and flag the discrepancy; check the current listing.

Does a quart count tell me how many people an appliance feeds?

Not by itself. The Emeril listing makes a 4–6 people claim, but serving size and cooking method vary; we have not tested portions.

Can I compare two baskets with one larger basket by total capacity?

You can compare the stated totals, but basket layout and usable cooking area differ. Check compartment size and food examples rather than treating the totals as equivalent.
