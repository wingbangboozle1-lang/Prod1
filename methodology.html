Method

How we verify, measure, and decide — without hallucinating

Automate what can be automated; put a gate in front of what cannot. This page is the machinery: evidence rules, analytics events, the empty campaign ledger, and the publishing cadence.

Evidence rule

  • Every product fact links to its Amazon listing and verification record. Missing = blank + flagged.
  • "Trending" / popularity claims require a dated snapshot from an official source. We make none; listing badges ("N bought in past month") are quoted only as dated listing snapshots.
  • Seller claims are attributed ("per listing"). Derived math shows its method. Prices always carry a snapshot date.
  • Where a listing contradicts itself, both values are published with the conflict flagged — never silently resolved (FLG-002, FLG-021, FLG-022).
  • Hands-on testing claims require a published protocol and photos. We make none.

Implementation: assets/app.js counts locally in localStorage and beacons to a configurable endpoint (data-analytics-endpoint on each page, currently empty). No third-party trackers, no cookies, no IPs stored. The self-hosted endpoint ships as code in analytics-endpoint/server.py (privacy notes in analytics-endpoint/README.md); deploying it is SG-03 at the review desk. Until then, counts are device-local and the ledger stays empty.

Publishing cadence & topic calendar

New guides are added to sitemap.xml and feed.xml at publish (both are generated by build_site.py with deterministic dates). The calendar: one question-shaped guide per week through the holiday window (SG-05), chosen from the approved clusters — air fryer choosing, thermometers, seasonal. The next topic after measurement starts comes from measured Search Console queries, not instinct. RSS feed: feed.xml. Sitemap: sitemap.xml.

Publishing gate

scripts/check_site.mjs runs in the Pages workflow before deploy and fails the build on: missing/disclosure-below-fold, Amazon links without rel="sponsored", broken internal links, duplicate or missing titles/descriptions, and banned claim wording ("bestseller", "top rated", "#1", "guaranteed", "most popular"). Automate the mechanical; gate the judgment.
