What fits under the cabinet? Checking counter height before you order

By The Countertop Guide editorial desk · Listing data checked 2026-11-19 · Spec-based, not hands-on tested how we work

Everyone measures the width. The number that ends a return is usually the height, because the cabinet above the counter does not move and the vent gap in the manual does. Here is how to do the check with the three fryers on this site, using only what their listings state.

On this page: The picks – Full comparison – Questions readers ask

The three numbers on the listing, and what each one governs

Height decides the cabinet. As listed: 10.63 in (27.0 cm) for the Chefman 6QT dual-basket, 10 in (25.4 cm) for the Chefman 8QT single basket, 14.8 in (37.6 cm) for the Emeril 26QT French-door. A 4 in difference between two machines is the difference between sliding one under a wall cabinet and leaving it out in the open.

Depth decides the walkway. The 6QT needs 13.38 in of depth, the 8QT 12.75 in, the Emeril 16.9 in. Depth is measured from the front of the box to the back, and it is the number that stops an appliance sitting proud of the counter edge.

Width decides the neighbours. 11.81 in, 13.5 in and 15.8 in respectively. Count what else lives on that stretch of counter before you fall for a bigger cavity.

Run the arithmetic yourself

Take the tape to three places and write the numbers down before you open a tab.

  1. The gap. Counter top to the bottom of the cabinet above. That is your ceiling.
  2. The stretch. Usable counter depth, minus whatever the wall outlet and its plug occupy at the back. A standard plug does not fold flat; a right-angle adapter is a real option, not an admission.
  3. The clearance the manual asks for. This is the number the listing does not carry. None of the three listings on this site states a required clearance above or behind the unit, so the check has to come from the manufacturer document that ships with it. We did not find one on the pages we captured - see Flagged for review.

Then add. If the Emeril is 14.8 in tall and your gap is 18 in, 3.2 in is what the cabinet leaves. If the manual asks for more than that behind and above, the machine does not live there, however good the capacity reads.

What the listing will not tell you about fit

Not captured on any of the three listings we read: weight, the length of the power cord, and the clearance envelope. Those decide whether a shelf holds it, whether the plug reaches, and whether the vents may sit against a wall. Two of the three are in the manual or in a call to the seller; none of them belongs in a buying article that has not measured them.

The thermometers are a different trap. Neither listing we captured carries dimensions at all, so the size of a handheld unit and its probe stays blank on our tables and is flagged. If a pocket thermometer matters to you, that is a question for the seller, not for a review page.

The honest order of operations

Measure first, then pick a form factor that fits, then compare capacity inside what fits. Most people run that list backwards, which is why air fryers are returned over a tape measure, not over cooking performance. The cleanup and counter-space guide does the footprint maths; the form-factor guide picks the shape.

Questions readers ask

How much counter height does an air fryer need?

Start with the listed height: 10 in, 10.63 in and 14.8 in for the three machines here. Then add whatever clearance the manual asks for above and behind, which the listings do not state. Your cabinet gap has to beat both numbers.

Do Amazon listings show required clearance?

Not on the three fryer listings we captured. Clearance appears in manufacturer documents, so treat the listing height as a floor, not as the answer.

Does an air fryer need space behind it?

Every appliance with vents needs room for airflow, but the figure is model-specific and is not on the listings we read. Check the manual that ships with the unit before you push it into a nook.
