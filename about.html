About

What this site is, and what it will not do

The Countertop Guide is a small buying publication for two kinds of countertop appliance: air fryers and meat thermometers. It exists because the comparison pages that rank for these products usually claim to have cooked with machines nobody cooked with. We do the opposite: we read the listing carefully, do the arithmetic the listing will not do for you, and say plainly which fields are missing.

Who writes it

Articles are published under the The Countertop Guide editorial desk byline. That is honest: no individual here has a credential we can show you, so we do not attach one. A named byline with a real bio is an open decision the site owner has to make — see SG-26.

How this site makes money

Product links are Amazon Associates links carrying our tracking ID (shopnow047c-20). If you click one and buy something on Amazon within the session rules, we earn a commission. That is the whole business model: no display advertising, no sponsored posts sold to brands, no email list, no third-party trackers.

Because we are paid on clicks, the interesting question is whether that payment changes what we tell you. Here is the contract we hold ourselves to:

  • Links go where they say they go. Every product link opens that exact Amazon listing. They carry rel="sponsored" so search engines can tell, and the word “sponsored” appears next to them for you.
  • We publish the negatives. Missing dimensions, an uncaptured wattage, a title that contradicts the bullet list — all of it is on the page, not in a basement. See Flagged for review.
  • No invented urgency. No countdowns, no “only 3 left”, no trend claim without a dated snapshot of the official category page.
  • No price theatre. We do not show a strike-through “list price” we cannot keep current. Amazon’s rules only allow a site to display price and availability when Amazon serves it or when it is pulled from their API and refreshed inside 24 hours; a static page can’t honestly do either, so the button says “see today’s price”.

How a fact gets onto a page

  1. A listing field is read and copied, with its URL and the date, into data/products.json.
  2. build_site.py renders it. Editorial lines such as “Good for” paraphrase a listed feature and attribute it; they never add a fact.
  3. scripts/check_meta.mjs runs in the deploy pipeline and fails the build if the disclosure, the sponsored marking, the metadata, a link or the voice rules are broken.
  4. Anything we could not confirm is blank on the page and present on the register, with the reason.

Corrections

Amazon listings change without notice, and a page that is 90 days old can be wrong through no fault of its author. The register is dated on purpose: every page states the date its data was checked. If you find a mismatch between this site and the current listing, the listing wins — and we want to know. Corrections belong on the register: the date, what it said, what it should say. No correction has been filed yet, so the first one opens the log.
