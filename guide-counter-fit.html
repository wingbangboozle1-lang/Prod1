Can a 26-quart oven actually fit on your counter? The depth math, in inches

By The Countertop Guide editorial desk · Listing data checked 2026-11-20 · Spec-based, not hands-on tested how we work

The Emeril 26QT French-door oven is the largest air fryer on this site by every measure that matters on a counter: deepest, widest, tallest and biggest footprint. Whether it fits yours is a tape-measure question, not a capacity question. This guide does the arithmetic from the listed dimensions only, and names the three things the listing does not tell you.

On this page: The picks – Full comparison – Questions readers ask

The numbers, as listed

The Emeril listing (B09B7SB46R) states 16.9 inches deep × 15.8 inches wide × 14.8 inches high (per listing). Converted with 1 inch = 2.54 cm, that is 42.9 cm deep × 40.1 cm wide × 37.6 cm high. The footprint is depth × width: 267.0 square inches. All three conversions are our arithmetic, not a manufacturer claim.

How to run the tape-measure test

  1. Measure your counter depth from backsplash to front edge, in inches. The oven needs 16.9 inches of depth before anything else is considered. If your counter is shallower, the oven overhangs or lives somewhere else.
  2. Measure your cabinet clearance from counter to the underside of the upper cabinet. The oven is 14.8 inches tall. The listing does not state a required clearance above the unit, so check the manual for the figure it asks for before you order.
  3. Measure your width. At 15.8 inches wide it is the widest unit on this page, and it is wider than it is deep. Leave room on both sides for the doors: the listing describes French doors that open with a single pull, but states no swing radius.
  4. Check the outlet. The listing’s Product information table states 1700 watts / 120 volts (per listing, read 2026-11-19). Dividing the two gives about 14.2 amps — our arithmetic, and close to the 15-amp figure the wattage guide works from. Read that guide for the circuit math and its limits; the manual, not this page, governs how you wire it up.

What the listing does not tell you

Three fit questions cannot be answered from the fields we captured, and this guide will not guess them. The required clearance behind and above the unit is not stated. The door-swing radius is not stated. The cord length and plug position are not stated. Each of those lives in the manual or product-support documents on the listing, or in a seller answer through the listing’s contact route. Until one of those is read, the honest sentence is “check the manual” — see Q-42.

If the depth does not fit

The two Chefman fryers are the fallback for a shallow counter, and the trade is capacity, not quality. The 8QT basket is 4.15 inches shallower than the Emeril (12.75 in against 16.9 in) and gives up the rotisserie and 12-inch pizza claims the Emeril listing makes (per listing). The 6QT dual-basket is 3.52 inches shallower and trades volume for two dishes at once. Depth is the number that ends most returns; measure it first.

Questions readers ask

Will the 26QT fit under standard cabinets?

The unit is 14.8 inches tall as listed. Whether that clears your cabinets depends on your clearance and on the clearance the manual requires, which the listing fields we captured do not state. Measure both before ordering.

How much counter depth does it need?

16.9 inches of depth as listed (42.9 cm derived), plus whatever clearance the manual asks for behind the unit. That figure is not in the fields we captured.

Is the footprint math a manufacturer claim?

No. Footprint and capacity-per-centimetre are our arithmetic from listed dimensions and title capacity. The listing states the dimensions; the ratios are derived and labelled as such.

What if my counter is too shallow?

The Chefman 8QT is 12.75 inches deep and the 6QT dual is 13.38 inches deep, both as listed. You trade the Emeril&rsquo;s rotisserie and pizza claims for a shallower footprint.
