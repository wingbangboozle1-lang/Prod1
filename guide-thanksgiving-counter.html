Thanksgiving counter math: what actually fits the week you cook for six?

By The Countertop Guide editorial desk · Listing data checked 2026-11-20 · Spec-based, not hands-on tested how we work

The oven is spoken for on 26 November, so the question is what a countertop appliance can be asked to do, and whether it physically fits where you would put it. Both answers are on the listing. Nobody here has cooked a turkey in any of these machines, so this is a fit-and-claim check, not a recipe.

On this page: The picks – Full comparison – Questions readers ask

The date, and why the counter is the real constraint

Thanksgiving Day 2026 is Thursday, 26 November. That is federal law (5 U.S.C. 6103), listed on the US Office of Personnel Management's holiday schedule and read on 2026-11-20. The Friday after and the Monday after carry retail names; neither is a holiday in that law, so treat them as days when the kitchen is also busy.

Ask two questions before you ask which machine cooks best. First: how much counter depth do you have where it will live, not where it will sit for one photograph? Second: what does this listing claim it can cook, in its own words? Everything below is those two questions applied to the three fryers on this site.

Floor area is our arithmetic (depth x width from the listing), not a manufacturer claim. The drawings on the product cards are ours too, drawn to the same scale so you can compare them by eye.

Read the depth column as the deciding number. Two of the three fit inside a shallow run of counter; the Emeril oven needs 16.9 in of it, which is why the sizing guide tells you to measure before the holiday, not after.

Height is the number people forget until the cabinet is full

The listed heights are 10.63 in, 14.8 in and 10 in. If the appliance has to live under a wall cabinet, that clearance, plus whatever space the manual asks for around vents and the door swing, is the constraint, not the width. None of the three listings states a required clearance in the fields we captured, so this site cannot tell you a safe number. Get a tape measure, and if the space is tight, ask the seller the clearance question in writing before the week the delivery window closes.

One more door-day detail: the Emeril's doors are French doors that "open with a single pull" per its bullets. Decide where the swing goes before you put a 16.9 in box in the corner.

Two 1700 W machines and one outlet

The Chefman 6QT listing states 1700 W at 120 V; the Emeril's Product information table states 1700 W at 120 V. On one small kitchen circuit, two of those plus a kettle is a question worth asking before Thanksgiving dinner, not during it. The 8QT is the awkward one: its bullets say 1000 W while its own spec table says 1700 W, and we publish both rather than choosing (FLG-022). The circuit arithmetic and what to ask an electrician live in the wattage and outlets guide.

Ten days out: the fit check, in order

  1. Measure the depth and the height of the spot it will live in. Write the two numbers down.
  2. Compare them against the stated size in the table above. If the appliance is deeper than the counter, stop there.
  3. Check what the listing says it cooks, in the listing's words. A capacity number in a title is not a meal plan.
  4. Read the variant selector before ordering. The 8QT comes in 6, 8 and 9-quart sizes and in two finishes per its own listing, and the photograph shows one of them.
  5. Decide who washes what. The 8QT's basket and tray are called top-rack dishwasher safe per bullets; the 6QT's baskets are called dishwasher safe per bullets; for the Emeril we captured no cleanup claim, so its accessories are a question for the listing, not for us.
  6. Confirm the power figure you will actually rely on, given the discrepancy above.

What this page cannot tell you

Not how long anything takes to cook. Not whether a 6-quart basket feeds your family. Not whether the machine is good. Those need a test kitchen and a protocol, and the decision on whether to build one is still open on the review desk (SG-09, Q-19). What this page can tell you is what the seller stated, when we read it, and what the listing leaves out. The gaps are listed on Flagged for review.

Questions readers ask

Can an air fryer replace an oven on Thanksgiving?

Nothing on these listings claims it can cook a whole turkey, and we are not going to invent that claim. The Emeril listing describes a rotisserie for a 6-lb chicken and a 12-inch pizza. Whether that helps your menu is your call, and it is a question the listing answers only about capacity, not about timing.

What size do I need to feed six people?

The only number on the listing that speaks to household size is the Emeril's bullet describing 26-quart capacity for four to six people. The other two state 6 and 8 quarts without a headcount. Do not read a headcount into a quart figure.

Is the deepest one also the biggest inside?

By the stated figures, yes on both counts for the Emeril: 16.9 in of depth and 26QT in its title. Note the title-to-field gap the register carries, 26QT against a 24.6 liter capacity field, which we show as two values rather than converting silently.

Do these listings say how much clearance they need?

Not in the fields captured for this register. That is why the fit check ends with a question for the seller instead of a number from us.
