Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

The listings we track include two basket-style air fryers and one French-door oven. Here is what to compare, and what this guide does not claim.

By the Editorial team (no named author; see flag P-13)

Two shapes, one job

The shape changes how you load food and how you reach it while it cooks.

The three air fryers in our table: the Chefman 6 Quart dual-basket air fryer oven (its title lists 2 independent 3Qt baskets), the Chefman 8 Qt TurboFry 4-in-1 air fryer, and the Emeril Lagasse 26QT French-door air fryer toaster oven. The first and last carry the word 'Oven' in their titles; the middle one does not.

Questions to ask

  • How much food do you cook per batch? Work this out from your own meals before you look at any number.
  • Do you need two foods cooked at once? The 6 Quart listing's title describes two baskets. Check the listing for whether settings are shared.
  • How many functions will you use each week? Count the functions the listing names. The 26QT title says '24 One-Touch Preset Options'; the 8 Qt title says '4-in-1'.
  • Will it fit where it will live? Measure your counter and the listing's dimensions. Dimensions are blank in our table because we did not capture them from an official field yet.
  • How will you clean it? Check the exact cleaning wording in the listing. Titles say the 6 Quart baskets are 'Dishwasher Safe' and the 8 Qt basket is 'Dishwasher-Safe'.

What this guide does not say

No product is ranked or recommended over another. No price, rating, review count, or popularity is stated. Listings change, so check the live listing before you buy.
