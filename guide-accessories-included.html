Air fryer accessories: which ones the listing actually includes

By The Countertop Guide editorial desk · Listing data checked 2026-11-20 · Spec-based, not hands-on tested how we work

The box is not the headline. A listing’s title, its bullets and its item-details table can describe three different sets of accessories - one of the listings on this site does exactly that. Here is where to look, in order, applied to the five products we track.

On this page: The picks – Full comparison – Questions readers ask

A worked example: six accessories or seven?

The Emeril 26QT’s title says “Six Cooking Accessories Included”. The Item-details table on the same listing describes a configuration with seven accessories. Both figures are on one page; both are quoted here with the date we read them ({VERIFIED}), and the disagreement stays visible as FLG-021 rather than being smoothed into one number. When a listing argues with itself, the safe move is not to pick the friendlier figure - it is to find the box list.

Where to look, in order

  1. The title. It is marketing text and the least precise field - but it is where “included” promises start.
  2. The bullets. Look for “in the box”, “includes” or included-components wording. The Chefman 8QT bullets name an 8-quart basket and a flat frying tray; the ThermoMaven’s included-components field names a calibration certificate.
  3. The Product information / Item-details table. This is where the Emeril lists accessories as configuration data - and where its count disagrees with the title.
  4. The manual or product PDFs linked from the listing. They often carry the definitive parts list. The Emeril’s warranty terms live exactly there, in a PDF, with no period stated on the page (FLG-005).
  5. Ask the seller through the listing if two fields still disagree after all of that. Keep the answer with your order record.

Included is not the same as covered

Two follow-up questions matter as much as the box list. First, care: the dishwasher claims above are the seller’s wording, and they differ by part (whole baskets on the 6QT; top rack only on the 8QT). Second, cover: the Chefman listings state a one-year assurance, the CHEF iQ states two years per its bullets, and the Emeril’s warranty period is not stated on the page at all - only a PDF link. We quote what each page states; we have not opened a box or filed a claim.

Accessories also wear out before appliances do. Before you buy a unit for its accessory count, check what a replacement part costs on the same listing - the count is only a deal if the parts stay available.

Questions readers ask

How many accessories come with the Emeril 26QT?

The title says six cooking accessories; the Item-details table on the same listing describes seven. We show both and flag the conflict (FLG-021) rather than choosing one. Check the In-the-box wording on the listing before you order.

Which parts are dishwasher safe?

Per the bullets: the Chefman 6QT's removable non-stick baskets, and the Chefman 8QT's basket and flat tray on the top rack. Confirm the exact wording on the listing you buy from - we have not washed any of them.

Does the ThermoMaven include anything besides the thermometer?

Its included-components field states one calibration certificate is included. What the certificate contains is not described in the fields we captured; check the listing or ask the seller.

What warranty do these accessories carry?

The Chefman listings state a one-year assurance each; the CHEF iQ states two years per its bullets. The Emeril page links a warranty PDF but states no period on the page (FLG-005).

Do the baskets count as accessories?

Yes - the Chefman 6QT lists two 3-quart baskets and the Chefman 8QT lists a basket and a flat frying tray. They are the removable, replaceable parts of these appliances.
