BUYING GUIDE · CAPACITY

A no-hype checklist for comparing air-fryer capacity

Compare air-fryer capacity by asking what the stated number means, how it maps to the food you cook, and whether a batch-based workflow suits your household.

First, check how the number is defined

Capacity figures are only useful when you know the unit, measurement basis, and context. Read the exact model’s official listing and owner manual. If one listing gives a volume and another describes a food quantity or usable area, do not treat the numbers as equivalent.

Capture the value exactly as stated, keep the unit, link the source, and note when you checked it. Leave it blank if the manufacturer does not clearly state it.

Describe your normal meal

Write down the food and portion you most often want to prepare. The question is not simply “How big is it?” but “Can I make this meal in the way I want, using the instructions for this exact model?” Consider food shape, arrangement, and whether you are comfortable cooking in batches.

  • What meal or ingredient do you have in mind?
  • How many portions do you usually need at once?
  • Would cooking in more than one round be acceptable?
  • Does the manual specify a loading or arrangement method?

Separate nominal size from usable cooking area

A headline capacity can hide practical differences in shape, access, or how food must be arranged. Verify the interior dimensions or basket/tray configuration only where official material provides them. Avoid converting dimensions into an estimated food capacity.

Look for manufacturer instructions on batch size and food placement. When these details are not supplied, record that they are unknown; do not substitute reviews or assumptions for the missing primary source.

Use the same test for every candidate

For each confirmed model, compare: the exact capacity wording and unit, interior configuration, official guidance for the food you make, stated exterior dimensions, and whether the number of batches fits your schedule. Use one row per model and attach an official source link for every populated specification.

A five-minute decision rule

Choose the smallest verified option that supports your recurring use without an inconvenient workflow, if minimizing footprint matters to you. Choose a larger verified option only when its documented configuration actually solves a recurring need and its counter footprint works. This is a decision rule, not a claim about any submitted product.

Keep the shortlist honest

Record only details supported by an official listing or manual for the exact model, along with the date checked. Leave unknown cells blank. If evidence is missing, pause the comparison rather than filling in the gap by inference.
