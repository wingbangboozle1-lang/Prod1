BUYING GUIDE · FORMAT

Basket or oven-style air fryer:
what should you compare?

The better format depends on what you plan to cook and how you move through the kitchen. Start with recurring jobs, then compare capacity, access, cleanup, and footprint using each model's official listing and manual.

Short answerThere is no universally better format. Write down two or three meals you make most often, how many portions you need at once, and where the appliance would live. Then compare the same tasks across the specific models you are considering.

1. What do you want it to do most?

Make a short list of repeat-use tasks before looking at feature badges. Are you mainly reheating, crisping a side, cooking a component alongside another meal, or replacing another appliance? This is a personal-use checklist, not a claim that any particular device performs those tasks well.

For every candidate, check its manufacturer instructions and official listing to confirm the function is supported. A format name or feature icon is not enough to establish how a particular model works.

2. Think about access and your cooking rhythm

Ask how you add food, check progress, turn or rearrange items, and remove the finished meal. Consider whether you normally cook one dish at a time or coordinate several components. Note any hand clearance, shelf access, or nearby counter obstruction that matters in your space.

  • What is the normal meal you have in mind?
  • Will you need to pause and rearrange food, or do you prefer to leave it alone?
  • Can you safely get food in and out where the unit will sit?
  • Does the official manual describe operation that suits your routine?

3. Compare capacity in a way that means something

Don't compare a capacity number alone. Check the stated unit and the manufacturer's description, then ask whether the usable cooking area suits the specific food and quantity you plan to prepare. If manufacturers describe capacity differently, don't treat the numbers as directly equivalent.

Our capacity checklist gives you a repeatable set of questions. For your own kitchen, pair it with the space and cleanup test.

4. Include cleanup, storage, and footprint

Find the care instructions for the exact model. Which parts are removable? What cleaning method does the manufacturer specify? Where will warm accessories cool, and where will they be stored? Measure the counter footprint and any ventilation or clearance requirements in the manual before deciding it can stay under a cabinet.

5. Use a simple side-by-side worksheet

For each candidate, record the exact model identifier, official listing and manual, the listed capacity with its unit, exterior dimensions, clearance instructions, removable parts, supported functions, and the date you checked. Leave any field blank until a primary source confirms it. If two candidates cannot be compared on the same basis, mark that limitation rather than guessing.

So, which format should you choose?

Choose the one whose verified instructions, usable capacity, access, cleaning routine, and dimensions best fit your recurring cooking jobs and kitchen. If neither fits cleanly, keep looking. A shortlist is useful only when the entries are identified and the evidence is comparable.
